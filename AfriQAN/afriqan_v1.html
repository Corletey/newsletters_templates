A Note From the Secretariat

Dear Members and Partners,

On 21 and 22 July 2026, the African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) convened its 14th General Assembly and Conference at the African Union Commission in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, bringing together quality assurance agencies, higher education leaders, and partners from across the continent and beyond.

The gathering marked a defining moment for the Network: a new Executive Board was elected to steer AfriQAN's next phase of growth, and two days of keynotes and panel discussions examined how quality assurance must evolve to meet the demands of digital transformation, emerging disciplines, and a more integrated African Higher Education and Research Space.

This edition brings you the full story from both days of the conference.

Day 1  -  21 July 2026

[Photo placeholder: 14th General Assembly, Addis Ababa]

AfriQAN Elects New Executive Board at its 14th General Assembly in Addis Ababa

The African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) held its 14th General Assembly on 21 July 2026 at the African Union Commission in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, bringing together quality assurance agencies and higher education stakeholders from across the continent. In his goodwill message, the Secretary General of the Association of African Universities (AAU) reflected on the progress of quality assurance in Africa since AfriQAN's founding in 2008, acknowledged the support of development partners including UNESCO and DAAD, and urged closer collaboration with the emerging Pan-African Quality Assurance and Accreditation Alliance (PAQAAA).

The Secretariat reported that AfriQAN now comprises 30 full and associate member institutions and encouraged members with outstanding dues to settle their subscriptions. Following a transparent electoral process, Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, Chief Executive Officer of the Council on Higher Education of Lesotho, was elected President; Prof. Joao Dias of Cabo Verde's Higher Education Regulatory Agency was elected Vice President by consensus; and Ms. Bertha Njembo of Namibia's National Council for Higher Education was confirmed to continue as Treasurer. The Assembly also called for stronger governance procedures, operational manuals, and financial reporting systems in the Network's next phase.

[Photo placeholder: Dr. Mary Lennon, INQAAHE keynote]

Quality Assurance Must Drive Transformation in Higher Education, Says INQAAHE President

Dr. Mary Lennon, President of the International Network for Quality Assurance Agencies in Higher Education (INQAAHE), delivered the conference's opening keynote on "Quality Assurance in Higher Education: Navigating Global Trends and Shaping the Future of Learning." She argued that quality assurance must evolve from a compliance exercise into a catalyst for transformation, driven by digital change, artificial intelligence, globalization, and shifting labour market demands. At its core, she said, quality assurance is about building trust between institutions, students, employers, and society.

Drawing on international experience, from Europe's mutual recognition frameworks to Asia-Pacific's risk-based accreditation and digital review systems, Dr. Lennon urged Africa to adapt rather than replicate external models while strengthening its own continental and regional initiatives. She identified integrating artificial intelligence into review processes, moving toward continuous quality assurance, and recognizing micro-credentials as priorities for the next generation of quality assurance systems, closing with a reminder that collaboration remains Africa's greatest strength.

[Photo placeholder: regional QA networks panel]

African Quality Assurance Networks Strengthen Collaboration and Harmonization Across the Continent

Representatives of RAFANAQ, SAQAN, EAQAN, RAQAES, and ANQAHE reaffirmed their shared commitment to quality, harmonization, and collaboration during a high-level conference session. Presentations highlighted a growing continental trend toward aligning with the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA) and the objectives of the HAQAA3 initiative, alongside increasing investment in capacity building, peer learning, and digital transformation.

SAQAN showcased strong governance and sustainable regional collaboration; EAQAN reported training more than 3,500 university staff and establishing national chapters across East Africa; RAQAES highlighted progress in regional cooperation; RAFANAQ reported advances in Francophone harmonization; and ANQAHE underscored its role in promoting exchange among Arab quality assurance agencies. All five networks cited limited and unsustainable funding and shortages of qualified professionals as shared challenges, and agreed to prioritize financial sustainability and deeper collaboration with continental initiatives.

[Photo placeholder: ASG-QA implementation session]

Advancing the Implementation of the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance

Conference participants reviewed progress in implementing the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA), a core component of the Pan-African Quality Assurance and Accreditation Framework (PAQAF). Speakers emphasized that harmonization is not about creating identical systems, but about building mutual trust and supporting recognition of qualifications and mobility across Africa.

Presenters showcased progress made through the HAQAA3 Initiative and the PAQAA Technical Unit, including agency reviews and capacity-building programmes that will inform a planned revision of the standards in 2027. A panel of national quality assurance agency experts stressed that quality assurance should be viewed as a continuous improvement process rather than a compliance exercise, calling for stronger governance, digital competencies among reviewers, and frameworks suited to online and blended learning.

Day 2  -  22 July 2026

[Photo placeholder: Prof. Olusola B. Oyewole keynote]

Quality Assurance Must Drive Excellence and Transformation in African Higher Education

Prof. Olusola B. Oyewole, Secretary General of the Association of African Universities (AAU), opened Day 2 with a keynote on "Quality Assurance in African Higher Education: Trends, Innovations, and Future Directions," calling on institutions to move beyond compliance and embrace quality assurance as a strategic tool for transformation. He stressed that universities must be assessed not only on adherence to standards, but on their contribution to national and continental development.

Prof. Oyewole highlighted Africa's progress in harmonizing quality assurance through the ASG-QA, the Pan-African Quality Assurance and Accreditation Framework (PAQAF), the Pan-African Quality Assurance Alliance (PAQAA), regional qualifications frameworks, and the African Credit Transfer System (ACTS), while cautioning that limited institutional capacity and resources remain significant challenges. He closed by calling for quality assurance systems that foster continuous improvement, innovation, and stronger collaboration to transform African higher education.

[Photo placeholder: emerging disciplines & future skills panel]

Quality Assurance Must Adapt to Emerging Disciplines and Future Skills

Experts from Kenya's Commission for University Education (CUE), Cabo Verde's Regulatory Agency for Higher Education (ARES), and Zambia's Higher Education Authority (HEA) called for quality assurance systems to evolve in response to rapid technological change and growing demand for future-ready graduates. The panel argued that traditional approaches centred on compliance and accreditation are no longer sufficient, urging a focus on graduate competencies, employability, adaptability, and innovation instead.

Panelists called for curricula that integrate interdisciplinary learning, artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity, sustainability, and entrepreneurship, supported by flexible learning pathways such as micro-credentials and recognition of prior learning. They stressed that regulatory agencies should move beyond compliance to become partners in institutional improvement, while adopting responsible AI practices and redesigning assessment methods to safeguard academic integrity.

[Photo placeholder: online & transnational education panel]

Strengthening Quality Assurance for Online and Transnational Education in Africa

Experts from the Southern African Quality Assurance Network (SAQAN), Egypt's National Authority for Quality Assurance and Accreditation of Education (NAQAAE), and the European Association for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ENQA) examined the rapid expansion of online and transnational education across Africa. Panelists noted that the COVID-19 pandemic significantly accelerated the shift, making digital and blended learning a permanent feature of higher education.

The discussion highlighted challenges including safeguarding academic integrity, verifying student identity, enhancing faculty capacity, and improving digital infrastructure, alongside ongoing regulatory gaps across the continent. The panel called for context-specific quality assurance standards, stronger governance of transnational education, and recognition of validated micro-credentials within national qualifications frameworks to protect the credibility of qualifications.

[Photo placeholder: IUCEA & CAMES panel on AHERS]

Quality Assurance Positioned as a Catalyst for the African Higher Education and Research Space

A panel featuring the Inter-University Council for East Africa (IUCEA) and CAMES discussed the role of quality assurance in advancing the African Higher Education and Research Space (AHERS). Speakers argued that quality assurance should move beyond a compliance-based approach to become a strategic instrument for building trust, promoting mutual recognition of qualifications, and facilitating academic mobility.

The panel highlighted the ASG-QA and the African Continental Qualifications Framework (ACQF) as essential tools for harmonization and integration, calling for stronger national systems, greater investment in institutional capacity, and sustainable financing. The session concluded that embedding a strong culture of quality is essential to enhancing teaching, research, innovation, and university-industry partnerships across the continent.

[Photo placeholder: Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, closing address]

AfriQAN President Calls for Collective Action to Transform Quality Assurance in Africa

Newly elected AfriQAN President Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele closed the conference with a call for collective action, describing quality assurance as a continuous culture of improvement that must evolve to meet the demands of digital transformation and technology-enhanced learning. He stressed the importance of modernizing quality assurance systems through innovation and continuous capacity building to keep African institutions globally competitive.

Presenting the incoming Executive Board's priorities, Dr. Letele pledged to strengthen AfriQAN's governance, deepen collaboration with continental quality assurance initiatives, expand membership, promote knowledge sharing and capacity building, and reinforce the AfriQAN Secretariat through a dedicated action plan. He called on quality assurance agencies, higher education institutions, governments, industry, and development partners to work together toward the aspirations of Agenda 2063, reminding participants that the conference's success would be measured by the actions taken afterwards.
