Welcome to the Inaugural Edition of the AfriQAN Newsletter

It is with great pleasure that the AfriQAN Secretariat, hosted by the Association of African Universities (AAU), welcomes you to the very first edition of the AfriQAN Newsletter.

This publication is born out of a simple conviction: that the work of strengthening quality assurance in African higher education is too important to be done in silence. It must be shared, discussed, and celebrated across the continent. As AfriQAN grows, so too must our collective voice, our mutual learning, and our shared commitment to excellence.

This inaugural edition captures a pivotal moment. It reports on the outcomes of our 14th General Assembly, held on 21 July 2026 at the African Union Commission in Addis Ababa - a gathering that elected a new Executive Board and reaffirmed the Network's strategic direction. It also documents the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference, where experts tackled pressing questions facing our sector: the quality of online and transnational education, the readiness of graduates for future skills and emerging disciplines, and the role of continental standards in building trust and recognition across borders.

But a newsletter is not merely a record of events. It is a tool for collective action. It is in that spirit that we highlight the words of our newly elected President, Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, whose closing address set a clear and ambitious agenda. Dr. Letele reminded us that quality assurance is not a static exercise but "a continuous culture of improvement" - one that must evolve to meet the demands of digital transformation and the changing realities of our higher education landscape. He pledged to strengthen governance, deepen collaboration with continental initiatives, expand membership, promote knowledge sharing, and reinforce the Secretariat. Most importantly, he called on all of us - quality assurance agencies, institutions, governments, industry, students, and development partners - to work together to achieve the aspirations of Agenda 2063 and build a strong culture of quality across the continent.

As Dr. Letele aptly put it, "the true success of the conference would be measured not by the discussions held, but by the actions implemented afterwards."

This newsletter is one such action - a commitment to transparency, knowledge sharing, and collective ownership of the AfriQAN agenda. We invite you to read, reflect, and engage. Share this edition with your colleagues, discuss the issues raised, and let us know how AfriQAN can better serve you and your institution.

The road ahead is long, but our collective resolve is strong. Together, we can build a credible, inclusive, and internationally competitive African higher education system - one that truly serves our learners, our societies, and our continent.

Thank you for your continued support and commitment to AfriQAN.

AfriQAN Annual Conference 2026 Advances the Future of Quality Assurance in African Higher Education

 

The African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) successfully hosted its 2026 Annual Conference, bringing together quality assurance professionals, higher education leaders, policymakers, development partners, researchers, and other stakeholders from across Africa and beyond to discuss the future of quality assurance in African higher education.

Held under the theme “Continental Collaboration for Quality Assurance in Africa,” the two-day conference – hosted on July 21-22, 2026, provided a dynamic platform for knowledge sharing, dialogue, and collaboration on emerging trends, innovations, and policy developments that are shaping higher education across the continent.

The conference featured keynote presentations, panel discussions, and interactive sessions that explored critical issues, including the evolving role of quality assurance, digital transformation, artificial intelligence, online and transnational education, emerging academic disciplines, curriculum innovation, and the importance of strengthening regional and continental cooperation.

AfriQAN Elects New Executive Board at its 14th General Assembly in Addis Ababa

 

The African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) has elected a new leadership team to steer the continental body through its next term, following the successful conclusion of its 14th General Assembly and Conference.

Dr. Joseph Letele of Lesotho assumes the role of President, with Prof. João da Silva of Cabo Verde as Vice President, and Ms. Bertha Njembo of Namibia serving as the Treasurer. The elections were conducted in a spirit of transparency and collaboration, reflecting the membership’s collective commitment to advancing quality assurance across African higher education.

The newly elected Board members bring a wealth of experience and deep expertise in quality assurance and higher education governance. Their appointment signals the confidence of the AfriQAN membership in their capacity to provide strategic direction, foster regional collaboration, and champion the Network’s mission of embedding a robust culture of quality across the continent.

African Quality Assurance Networks Deepen Collaboration to Advance Higher Education Excellence Across the Continent
 

Regional quality assurance networks are strengthening collaboration to promote greater consistency and trust in African higher education. At the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference and General Assembly in Addis Ababa, representatives from regional networks shared experiences and strategies for strengthening quality assurance, while highlighting the importance of aligning regional efforts with the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA) and initiatives such as HAQAA3.

The discussions also identified shared challenges, including sustainable funding, the availability of qualified quality assurance professionals, differing regulatory frameworks, and the growing impact of online learning and digital transformation. Participants reaffirmed the importance of stronger partnerships, capacity building and continued collaboration to advance quality assurance across the continent.

Safeguarding Quality in Africa’s Expanding Online and Transnational Education
 

The growth of online and transnational education is expanding access and flexibility across Africa, while raising new questions about quality, academic standards and the credibility of qualifications. At the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference and General Assembly, experts examined how quality assurance systems can respond to these changing modes of education.

Discussions highlighted the need for effective governance, prepared faculty, reliable technology and appropriate quality assurance mechanisms. Participants also considered challenges including academic integrity, digital infrastructure and the delivery of practical learning online. As online education continues to evolve alongside artificial intelligence, learning analytics and micro-credentials, quality assurance frameworks will need to remain responsive to safeguard trust and ensure credible learning experiences.

Quality Assurance Systems Must Evolve to Prepare Graduates for Future Skills and Emerging Disciplines
 

African higher education institutions are being encouraged to rethink quality assurance to better prepare graduates for a rapidly changing world of work. At the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference and General Assembly, experts examined how quality assurance can support emerging disciplines and future skills, with emphasis on graduate competencies, employability, adaptability and innovation.

Discussions highlighted the need for stronger curricula, flexible learning pathways and closer collaboration with employers and professional bodies. Emerging areas such as artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity and sustainability are creating new demands, while micro-credentials and lifelong learning are reshaping educational pathways. Participants also stressed the importance of adapting quality assurance to support responsible use of artificial intelligence while maintaining academic integrity and authentic learning.

Advancing Quality and Trust in African Higher Education: The Role of Continental Quality Assurance Standards
 

The African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA) provide a common continental framework for strengthening quality, building trust and supporting the recognition of qualifications across Africa. Discussions at the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference highlighted the importance of implementing the standards as part of a culture of continuous improvement, while responding to developments in digital and blended education.

Implementation is supported through initiatives including HAQAA3 and the PAQAA Technical Unit, with the Association of African Universities contributing to quality assurance coordination, reviews and capacity building. As the ASG-QA is expected to be revised in 2027, continued adaptation will be important to ensure that quality assurance frameworks remain responsive to changing models of higher education.

AfriQAN President Calls for Collective Action to Transform Quality Assurance in Africa

 

Newly elected AfriQAN President, Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, has called for collective action to strengthen quality assurance across African higher education. Closing the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference and General Assembly, he emphasised the need for quality assurance to evolve alongside digital transformation, technology-enabled learning and emerging developments in higher education.

Under the new Executive Board, AfriQAN will focus on strengthening governance, expanding membership, promoting knowledge sharing and capacity building, and reviewing quality assurance frameworks. Dr. Letele also called for greater collaboration among quality assurance agencies, higher education institutions, governments, industry, students and development partners to strengthen a culture of quality and support the goals of Agenda 2063.
