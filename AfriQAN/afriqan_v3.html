Welcome to the Inaugural Edition of the AfriQAN Newsletter

It is with great pleasure that the AfriQAN Secretariat, hosted by the Association of African Universities (AAU), welcomes you to the very first edition of the AfriQAN Newsletter.

This publication is born out of a simple conviction: that the work of strengthening quality assurance in African higher education is too important to be done in silence. It must be shared, discussed, and celebrated across the continent. As AfriQAN grows, so too must our collective voice, our mutual learning, and our shared commitment to excellence.

This inaugural edition captures a pivotal moment. It reports on the outcomes of our 14th General Assembly, held on 21 July 2026 at the African Union Commission in Addis Ababa - a gathering that elected a new Executive Board and reaffirmed the Network's strategic direction. It also documents the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference, where experts tackled pressing questions facing our sector: the quality of online and transnational education, the readiness of graduates for future skills and emerging disciplines, and the role of continental standards in building trust and recognition across borders.

But a newsletter is not merely a record of events. It is a tool for collective action. It is in that spirit that we highlight the words of our newly elected President, Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, whose closing address set a clear and ambitious agenda. Dr. Letele reminded us that quality assurance is not a static exercise but "a continuous culture of improvement" - one that must evolve to meet the demands of digital transformation and the changing realities of our higher education landscape. He pledged to strengthen governance, deepen collaboration with continental initiatives, expand membership, promote knowledge sharing, and reinforce the Secretariat. Most importantly, he called on all of us - quality assurance agencies, institutions, governments, industry, students, and development partners - to work together to achieve the aspirations of Agenda 2063 and build a strong culture of quality across the continent.

As Dr. Letele aptly put it, "the true success of the conference would be measured not by the discussions held, but by the actions implemented afterwards."

This newsletter is one such action - a commitment to transparency, knowledge sharing, and collective ownership of the AfriQAN agenda. We invite you to read, reflect, and engage. Share this edition with your colleagues, discuss the issues raised, and let us know how AfriQAN can better serve you and your institution.

The road ahead is long, but our collective resolve is strong. Together, we can build a credible, inclusive, and internationally competitive African higher education system - one that truly serves our learners, our societies, and our continent.

Thank you for your continued support and commitment to AfriQAN.

AfriQAN Elects New Executive Board at its 14th General Assembly in Addis Ababa

[Photo placeholder: AfriQAN Elects New Executive Board at its 14th General Assembly in Addis Ababa]
 

The African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) successfully held its 14th General Assembly on 21 July 2026 at the African Union Commission (AUC), Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, bringing together representatives of quality assurance agencies and higher education stakeholders from across the continent. The meeting marked an important milestone in strengthening continental collaboration for quality assurance in higher education and setting the strategic direction for the Network's next phase of growth.

The General Assembly formally opened with the Executive Board and the AAU Secretary General present in accordance with the AfriQAN Constitution. Delegates adopted the agenda and proceeded to deliberations centered on the future of quality assurance in African higher education.

In his goodwill message, Prof. Olusola Oyewole, AAU Secretary General, reflected on the remarkable progress of quality assurance in Africa, recalling that only a handful of African countries had established quality assurance systems in 2006. He highlighted the establishment of AfriQAN in 2008 following consultations among African quality assurance agencies and acknowledged the valuable contributions of development partners, including UNESCO and DAAD, in advancing quality assurance across the continent. He emphasized the continued importance of quality assurance in enhancing higher education and graduate competencies and encouraged closer collaboration between AfriQAN and the emerging Pan-African Quality Assurance Alliance (PAQAA). He further urged the incoming leadership to prioritize sustainability, resource mobilization, and institutional strengthening.

In his welcome remarks, Prof. Charles Kihampa, the outgoing AfriQAN President welcomed delegates and expressed appreciation to the AAU Secretariat for its unwavering support throughout the Executive's tenure. He reviewed the Network's achievements despite operational challenges and acknowledged improvements in communication and coordination following the establishment of the current Secretariat. He also encouraged members to strengthen their commitment to AfriQAN through active participation and timely payment of membership subscriptions.

The Secretariat presented an update on the Network's membership, reporting that AfriQAN currently comprises 30 full and associate member institutions. While recognizing members that are up to date with their subscriptions, the Secretariat encouraged institutions with outstanding dues to settle their membership fees to support the sustainability and growth of the Network.

The Assembly also engaged in extensive discussions on strengthening AfriQAN's governance framework. Members emphasized the need for the development of Standard Operating Procedures, operational and financial management guidelines, annual Executive handover reports, improved audit arrangements, enhanced membership subscription mechanisms, and strengthened Secretariat support. The outgoing Executive recommended that the incoming Board prioritize the development of governance procedures, operational manuals, stronger financial reporting systems, and improved member engagement.

The General Assembly expressed its sincere appreciation to the Association of African Universities for hosting and supporting the AfriQAN Secretariat throughout the reporting period. Members acknowledged that AAU staff had provided Secretariat services without charging the Network and approved a token of appreciation in recognition of this valuable contribution.

One of the key highlights of the General Assembly was the election of a new Executive Board to lead the Network. Following a transparent electoral process, Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, Chief Executive Officer of the Council on Higher Education of Lesotho, was elected President of AfriQAN. Prof. João Dias of the Higher Education Regulatory Agency of Cabo Verde was elected Vice President by consensus, while Ms. Bertha Njembo of the National Council for Higher Education of Namibia was confirmed to continue serving as Treasurer.

In his acceptance speech, the newly elected President thanked members for the confidence reposed in him and pledged to strengthen governance, enhance resource mobilization, expand membership engagement, and build on the achievements of the outgoing Executive. He called on all members to work collectively towards advancing quality assurance and excellence in African higher education.

The General Assembly concluded with a vote of thanks to the outgoing Executive Board, the AAU Secretariat, and all delegates for their active participation. Members reaffirmed their shared commitment to strengthening AfriQAN as the continent's leading network for promoting quality assurance in higher education and fostering collaboration among quality assurance agencies across Africa.

African Quality Assurance Networks Deepen Collaboration to Advance Higher Education Excellence Across the Continent

[Photo placeholder: African Quality Assurance Networks Deepen Collaboration to Advance Higher Education Excellence Across the Continent]
 

Regional quality assurance networks across Africa are strengthening collaboration to build greater trust and consistency within the continent’s higher education systems. This shared commitment was reinforced at a high-level session in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, as part of the 2026 African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) Annual Conference and General Assembly.

The session brought together representatives from the African Francophone Network of National Quality Assurance Agencies (RAFANAQ), the Southern African Quality Assurance Network (SAQAN), the East African Quality Assurance Network (EAQAN), the Réseau Africain des Responsables des Organismes d’Assurance Qualité de l’Enseignement Supérieur (RAQAES), and the Arab Network for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ANQAHE). Participants shared experiences, achievements, and strategies for strengthening quality assurance across their respective regions while contributing to the broader vision of a harmonised African Higher Education Area, which seeks to promote greater comparability and recognition of qualifications across African countries.

A key theme throughout the discussions was the growing alignment of regional quality assurance systems with the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA). Discussions also highlighted the objectives of the Harmonisation of African Higher Education Quality Assurance and Accreditation (HAQAA3) Initiative, in which the Association of African Universities (AAU) plays a key role in advancing quality assurance and harmonisation efforts across the continent.

Speakers emphasised that adopting common quality assurance principles while respecting regional and national contexts is essential for building trust, facilitating the recognition of qualifications, and supporting student and staff mobility across Africa.

The session highlighted significant progress made by the regional networks in advancing quality assurance. SAQAN showcased its strong governance structures and sustainable regional collaboration, while EAQAN reported training more than 3,500 university staff and establishing national chapters to strengthen quality assurance practices across East Africa. RAFANAQ highlighted its efforts to enhance cooperation among Francophone countries, whereas RAQAES outlined progress in governance and regional coordination. ANQAHE also shared its work in promoting cooperation, knowledge exchange, and the dissemination of good practices among quality assurance agencies in the Arab region.

Despite these achievements, participants acknowledged several common challenges affecting quality assurance systems across the continent. These include limited and unsustainable funding, shortages of qualified quality assurance professionals, differences in national regulatory frameworks, and the need to adapt quality assurance approaches to emerging trends such as online learning, artificial intelligence, and digital transformation. The shared nature of these challenges further underscores the importance of regional cooperation and the exchange of practical solutions across networks.

Speakers agreed that addressing these challenges will require stronger partnerships, increased investment in institutional capacity, and sustained collaboration among regional networks, governments, higher education institutions, and development partners. They also underscored the importance of sharing expertise and best practices to strengthen the resilience and effectiveness of quality assurance systems across Africa.

Looking ahead, the networks committed to prioritizing financial sustainability, expanding training and professional development initiatives, accelerating digital transformation, strengthening the implementation of the ASG-QA, and deepening collaboration with continental quality assurance initiatives and bodies responsible for the recognition of qualifications. These efforts, participants noted, will help create more coherent and responsive quality assurance systems that support excellence, innovation, and the international competitiveness of African higher education.

Beyond the regional networks featured in the session, the 2026 AfriQAN Annual Conference brought together quality assurance agencies, university leaders, policymakers, and development partners from across Africa to explore strategies for strengthening quality assurance and fostering greater cooperation within the continent's higher education sector.

The discussions reinforced the important role of regional quality assurance networks in advancing Africa's higher education agenda. By harmonising standards, sharing expertise, and strengthening cooperation across borders, these networks can help build greater trust in African higher education and ensure that qualifications and standards are more consistently recognised across institutions and countries.

Safeguarding Quality in Africa’s Expanding Online and Transnational Education

[Photo placeholder: Safeguarding Quality in Africa’s Expanding Online and Transnational Education]
 

The rapid expansion of online and transnational education is reshaping higher education across Africa, creating new opportunities for access, flexibility, and lifelong learning while raising important questions about quality, credibility, and academic standards.

As institutions increasingly embrace digital and cross-border models of education, ensuring that these modes of delivery provide credible and meaningful learning experiences has become an important priority for higher education institutions and quality assurance bodies. How can the quality of online education be maintained? How can the credibility of qualifications obtained through digital platforms be protected? And how can institutions ensure that students receive comparable learning experiences, regardless of where or how they study?

These questions were at the centre of discussions during the 2026 African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) Annual Conference and General Assembly, hosted with the support of the Association of African Universities (AAU). Experts from the Southern African Quality Assurance Network (SAQAN), Egypt’s National Authority for Quality Assurance and Accreditation of Education (NAQAAE), and the European Association for Quality Assurance in Higher Education (ENQA) examined the evolving role of quality assurance in supporting digital and transnational education, including programmes delivered across national borders.

The discussions highlighted an important distinction between simply transferring traditional education online and creating a quality online learning experience. Online education is not just about placing lectures, reading materials, or assessments on digital platforms. It requires thoughtful planning, effective governance, trained faculty, reliable technology, and quality assurance mechanisms that respond to the realities of digital learning.

The Evolution of Online Education

Although online education developed from traditional distance learning models, its growth accelerated significantly during the COVID-19 pandemic, as institutions across Africa and around the world quickly adopted digital learning solutions to ensure educational continuity. What expanded rapidly as a response to an emergency has now become a permanent feature of higher education.

Today, online and blended learning models continue to expand, supported by emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, learning analytics, and stackable micro-credentials. These developments provide exciting opportunities for institutions to reach more learners, support flexible learning pathways, and respond to the growing demand for continuous skills development.

But with innovation comes responsibility. Quality assurance systems must evolve alongside these changes to ensure that technological advancement does not compromise academic standards. The question is no longer whether online education should exist, but rather how institutions can ensure that it is delivered effectively, ethically, and with the same commitment to quality expected from all forms of higher education.

The Quality Assurance Challenge

Quality assurance for online and transnational education requires a different approach. Traditional quality assurance methods may not always address the unique realities of digital learning environments.

Several areas require careful consideration. Academic integrity remains a major concern. Institutions must develop reliable approaches to protect examinations, verify student identity, and ensure that assessments accurately measure learning outcomes.

Faculty readiness is another important factor. Teaching online requires more than digital access. Academic staff need the skills and support to design engaging virtual learning experiences, use digital tools effectively, and maintain meaningful interaction with students.

Infrastructure also plays a critical role. While internet connectivity and digital access continue to improve across Africa, unequal access to technology, connectivity limitations, and varying levels of digital readiness can affect the quality and inclusiveness of online education.

There is also the question of practical learning. Programmes that require laboratory work, field experience, or hands-on training must explore innovative ways to maintain quality within virtual environments.

Building Responsive Quality Assurance Frameworks

A strong quality assurance system must be flexible enough to respond to changing educational models while maintaining clear standards. The discussions highlighted the need for context-specific quality assurance approaches that consider Africa’s diverse higher education landscape. There is no single solution that can address every institution, country, or learning environment.

Quality assurance frameworks must be developed in ways that recognise local realities while aligning with international expectations.

This includes strengthening governance structures for transnational education, ensuring that institutions offering cross-border programmes meet appropriate quality standards, and creating mechanisms for recognising emerging forms of learning, such as validated micro-credentials, within national qualifications frameworks.

This focus aligns with the broader work of the AAU in advancing quality assurance and harmonisation within African higher education.

These steps are important as education becomes more flexible and interconnected. Learners are seeking opportunities that allow them to acquire knowledge and skills throughout their lives, and higher education systems must be prepared to support these changing needs.

Protecting Trust in African Higher Education

At the centre of quality assurance is trust. Students need confidence that the education they receive will provide value. Employers need assurance that qualifications represent relevant knowledge and skills. Institutions need reliable systems that support collaboration and recognition across borders.

Online and transnational education present Africa with significant opportunities to expand access, promote lifelong learning, and strengthen collaboration across borders. But access alone is not enough. The quality of learning experiences and the credibility of qualifications must remain a priority.

Strengthening quality assurance for digital and cross-border education will require institutions, quality assurance agencies, policymakers, and stakeholders to continuously adapt and improve. The goal is not to limit innovation but to guide it; not to create barriers, but to build confidence.

As higher education continues to evolve, Africa’s quality assurance systems must evolve with it to ensure every learner, regardless of location or mode of study, has access to quality, credible, and meaningful education.

Quality Assurance Systems Must Evolve to Prepare Graduates for Future Skills and Emerging Disciplines

[Photo placeholder: Quality Assurance Systems Must Evolve to Prepare Graduates for Future Skills and Emerging Disciplines]
 

Preparing Africa’s graduates for a rapidly changing world of work requires more than introducing new programmes. It requires rethinking how the quality and relevance of education are assessed to ensure that graduates develop the knowledge, skills, and adaptability needed to respond to changing labour market demands.

This challenge was the focus of a panel discussion on “Quality Assurance of Emerging Disciplines and Future Skills Programmes” at the 2026 African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) Annual Conference and General Assembly held on July 21-22, 2026, in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Experts from Kenya’s Commission for University Education (CUE), Cabo Verde’s Regulatory Agency for Higher Education (ARES), and Zambia’s Higher Education Authority (HEA), called for quality assurance systems across Africa to evolve beyond an emphasis on regulatory compliance and give greater attention to equipping graduates with the knowledge, skills, and competencies needed to thrive in a rapidly changing world of work.

The discussion reflects a broader priority for African higher education and aligns with AfriQAN’s commitment to advancing quality and relevance across the continent. As skills requirements continue to change, quality assurance will be critical not only in maintaining academic standards but also in ensuring that higher education responds effectively to Africa’s development needs.

Against this backdrop, experts called on higher education institutions to respond to these changes by redesigning curricula, adopting flexible learning pathways, strengthening internal quality assurance systems, and deepening collaboration with employers, professional bodies, and other stakeholders.

A more impact-driven approach to quality assurance was identified as central to this transformation. Contributors stressed the importance of graduate competencies, employability, adaptability, innovation, and societal contribution. Quality assurance frameworks, they noted, should go beyond assessing whether institutions meet regulatory requirements and should also examine how effectively universities prepare students to respond to evolving economic, technological, and social challenges.

Curriculum relevance was another key priority highlighted during the discussion. Universities were encouraged to redesign curricula to incorporate emerging areas such as artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity, sustainability, entrepreneurship, and communication skills. Experts also advocated for flexible learning pathways, including micro-credentials, lifelong learning opportunities, and recognition of prior learning, to ensure that programmes remain responsive to changing skills needs.

Strengthening internal quality assurance systems within higher education institutions was equally emphasised. Universities were encouraged to take greater responsibility for graduate outcomes by developing innovative teaching approaches, improving learning experiences, and working closely with employers, professional bodies, and other stakeholders to align academic programmes with industry and societal needs.

The role of regulatory agencies must also evolve. Experts pointed to the need for regulators to move from being primarily compliance-focused bodies to becoming partners in institutional improvement and innovation. By providing guidance, supporting reforms, and encouraging collaboration, regulators can help institutions develop quality systems that respond effectively to emerging challenges and opportunities.

Artificial intelligence was identified as both an opportunity and a challenge for higher education. While AI offers new possibilities for teaching, learning, and research, contributions stressed the need for responsible adoption, particularly in the redesign of assessment methods to protect academic integrity while encouraging authentic learning and creativity among students.

The session concluded that a flexible, forward-looking, and outcomes-focused quality assurance system is essential for preparing Africa’s graduates for the future of work. Through stronger institutional systems, innovative curricula, and collaboration among universities, regulators, employers, and other stakeholders, higher education institutions can play a critical role in supporting sustainable development across the continent.

Advancing Quality and Trust in African Higher Education: The Role of Continental Quality Assurance Standards

[Photo placeholder: Advancing Quality and Trust in African Higher Education: The Role of Continental Quality Assurance Standards]
 

Strengthening quality assurance in African higher education is critical to building trust, improving academic systems, and ensuring that qualifications are recognised and valued across borders. This effort aligns closely with the AfriQAN’s target to advance quality and relevance across the continent, particularly through its support for initiatives that strengthen quality assurance and promote greater harmonisation in African higher education institutions. As higher education continues to evolve, effective quality assurance will be increasingly important in maintaining academic standards while supporting greater confidence and cooperation across the continent.

The African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA) were developed to provide a common framework for strengthening quality assurance practices across the continent. They serve as a shared reference point for higher education institutions and quality assurance agencies, supporting the development of systems that are credible, transparent, and responsive to the changing needs of African higher education.

However, harmonisation does not mean that every country or institution must adopt identical approaches. There is a difference between creating uniformity and creating alignment. The goal of the ASG-QA is to encourage mutual trust among higher education systems while respecting the unique national and regional contexts within which institutions operate.

This distinction is important because Africa’s higher education landscape is diverse. Institutions differ in their structures, policies, resources, and priorities. The ASG-QA provides a foundation that allows these differences to coexist while promoting common principles of quality, accountability, and continuous improvement.

From Framework to Implementation

The effectiveness of any quality assurance framework depends largely on its implementation. Over the years, efforts have been made to move the ASG-QA from a guiding document into a practical tool that institutions and agencies can use to improve their systems.

Through initiatives such as the Harmonisation of African Higher Education Quality Assurance and Accreditation (HAQAA3) Initiative and the Pan African Quality Assurance Alliance (PAQAA) Technical Unit, significant progress has been recorded in supporting the implementation of the ASG-QA. The AAU plays a key role in advancing these initiatives, particularly through coordinating and supporting quality assurance activities, institutional and agency reviews, capacity-building programmes and training designed to strengthen the skills and knowledge of quality assurance professionals across Africa. These efforts have included institutional and agency reviews, capacity-building programmes, and training activities designed to strengthen the skills and knowledge of quality assurance professionals across Africa.

These initiatives have helped institutions and agencies better understand how the standards can be applied within their respective environments. They have also provided opportunities to share experiences, identify challenges, and develop approaches that respond to the realities of African higher education.

Implementation is not a one-time activity. It requires continuous reflection, adaptation, and improvement. As higher education continues to evolve, quality assurance systems must also evolve to address emerging trends such as digital transformation, online learning, blended education, and new models of teaching and learning.

Quality Assurance as a Culture of Improvement

Effective implementation also requires a shift from viewing quality assurance primarily as a compliance exercise towards embedding it as a culture of continuous improvement. Quality assurance should not only be about meeting minimum requirements or preparing for external assessments. It should be seen as a continuous process that helps institutions identify areas for improvement, strengthen academic practices, and enhance the overall student experience.

A strong quality assurance culture requires commitment from all stakeholders. Institutional leaders, academic staff, students, policymakers, and quality assurance agencies all have important roles to play in ensuring that quality becomes embedded within everyday operations.

Strong governance structures, stakeholder involvement, evidence-based decision-making, and effective monitoring systems are essential components of successful quality assurance implementation. When these elements work together, institutions are better positioned to provide education that meets both local and international expectations.

Supporting Mobility and Recognition of Qualifications

One of the major benefits of implementing the ASG-QA is its contribution to improving the recognition of qualifications across Africa.

A trusted and harmonised quality assurance system creates confidence among institutions, employers, governments, and students. It supports greater mobility by making it easier for qualifications obtained in one African country to be understood and recognised in another.

This is particularly important as Africa continues to pursue stronger regional integration and collaboration in higher education. Improved recognition of qualifications can open more opportunities for students, researchers, and professionals while encouraging partnerships among institutions across the continent.

Preparing for the Future of African Higher Education

Looking ahead, the ASG-QA must remain responsive to emerging realities in African Higher education.

The planned revision of the standards in 2027 presents an opportunity to reflect on lessons learned from implementation and ensure that the framework remains relevant. Emerging areas such as digital learning, technological advancement, and changing education models will require quality assurance systems that are flexible and forward-looking.

These priorities were also reflected in discussions at the 2026 African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) Annual Conference and General Assembly, where contributors examined the implementation of the ASG-QA and the need for quality assurance systems to remain responsive to changes within African higher education. The discussions reinforced the importance of sustained institutional commitment and collaboration in translating continental standards into practice.

The future of African higher education depends not only on expanding access but also on ensuring that the education provided is of high quality, internationally credible, and aligned with the needs of society.

The implementation of the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance represents an important step towards strengthening higher education systems across Africa. By translating these standards into practice, institutions and quality assurance agencies can build greater trust, promote continuous improvement, and strengthen cooperation across the continent.

AfriQAN President Calls for Collective Action to Transform Quality Assurance in Africa

[Photo placeholder: AfriQAN President Calls for Collective Action to Transform Quality Assurance in Africa]
 

The newly elected President of the African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN), Dr. Moeketsi Joseph Letele, concluded the conference with a strong call for collective action to strengthen quality assurance as a driver of excellence and transformation in African higher education.

In his closing address, Dr. Letele emphasized that quality assurance is a continuous culture of improvement that must evolve to meet the demands of digital transformation, technology-enhanced learning, and emerging trends in higher education. He stressed the importance of modernizing quality assurance systems through innovation and continuous capacity building to ensure African institutions remain globally competitive.

The President highlighted the need to develop harmonized quality assurance frameworks, including African Standards and Guidelines for Transnational Higher Education, to promote trust, facilitate the recognition of qualifications, support student mobility, and advance the African Higher Education and Research Space.

Presenting the priorities of the newly elected Executive Board, Dr. Letele pledged to strengthen AfriQAN's governance, deepen collaboration with continental quality assurance initiatives, expand membership, promote knowledge sharing and capacity building, review key institutional frameworks, and reinforce the AfriQAN Secretariat through a dedicated action plan.

He further called on quality assurance agencies, higher education institutions, governments, industry, students, and development partners to work together to achieve the aspirations of Agenda 2063 and build a strong culture of quality across the continent.

Concluding his remarks, Dr. Letele reminded participants that the true success of the conference would be measured not by the discussions held, but by the actions implemented afterwards. He expressed appreciation to the African Union, development partners, the outgoing Executive Board, the AfriQAN Secretariat, speakers, and participants for their contributions to a successful conference and encouraged all stakeholders to continue working together to build a credible, inclusive, and internationally competitive African higher education system.
