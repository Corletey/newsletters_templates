Dear Colleagues and Stakeholders,
We are pleased to share highlights from the African Quality Assurance Network (AfriQAN) 14th General Assembly and Annual Conference, held in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. The gathering brought together quality assurance professionals, higher education leaders, and stakeholders from across the continent under the theme of continental collaboration for quality assurance in African higher education.

DAY 1 - GENERAL ASSEMBLY
1. Election of New Executive Board
[Photo: Newly Elected Executive Board Members]
The General Assembly marked a significant transition in AfriQAN's leadership with the election of a new Executive Board. Delegates from member quality assurance agencies across the continent participated in the democratic process, which will guide the Network's strategic direction for the coming term. The newly constituted Board reflects AfriQAN's commitment to regional representation and institutional diversity in its governance structures.
2. INQAAHE Keynote Address
[Photo: INQAAHE Keynote Speaker Addressing Delegates]
A representative from the International Network for Quality Assurance Agencies in Higher Education (INQAAHE) delivered a keynote address that situated AfriQAN's work within the global quality assurance landscape. The address highlighted opportunities for African quality assurance agencies to engage with international frameworks while maintaining responsiveness to continental and national contexts. Emphasis was placed on the role of regional networks in strengthening global quality assurance architecture.
3. Regional Quality Assurance Network Collaboration
[Photo: Regional QA Network Representatives in Discussion]
A dedicated session brought together representatives from Africa's regional quality assurance networks to explore mechanisms for deeper collaboration. Discussions focused on harmonisation of standards, mutual recognition agreements, and joint capacity-building initiatives. Participating networks shared experiences and identified concrete areas for coordinated action in the year ahead.
4. ASG-QA Implementation Progress
[Photo: ASG-QA Working Session]
The African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA) implementation featured prominently in the Assembly's deliberations. Member agencies reported on progress in domesticating the continental standards within their national quality assurance frameworks. The session provided a platform for peer learning, with agencies at different stages of implementation sharing practical insights and challenges encountered.
5. AAU Secretary General's Keynote
[Photo: AAU Secretary General Delivering Keynote Address]
The Secretary General of the Association of African Universities (AAU) delivered a keynote that underscored the centrality of quality assurance to the realisation of the African Union's Agenda 2063 and the Continental Education Strategy for Africa. The address reaffirmed AAU's commitment to hosting and supporting AfriQAN as the continent's premier platform for quality assurance coordination and capacity development.
DAY 2 - CONFERENCE SESSIONS
6. Quality Assurance in Emerging Disciplines
[Photo: Panel on Emerging Disciplines]
This panel examined the quality assurance challenges posed by newly emerging academic disciplines and interdisciplinary programmes. Panellists from diverse institutional contexts discussed approaches to developing standards for fields that do not fit neatly within traditional disciplinary frameworks. The conversation highlighted the need for flexible yet rigorous quality assurance mechanisms that can accommodate innovation in curriculum design.
7. Online and Transnational Education Panel
[Photo: Online and Transnational Education Panel Session]
A panel of experts addressed the growing prevalence of online and transnational education delivery models across Africa. Discussions centred on quality assurance frameworks for cross-border provision, the regulation of digital learning platforms, and the protection of students enrolled in transnational programmes. Participants explored how existing continental instruments, including the Addis Convention, can be leveraged to strengthen oversight in this rapidly evolving space.
8. AHERS Panel Discussion
[Photo: AHERS Panel Discussion]
The African Higher Education Research and Scholarship (AHERS) panel brought together researchers and quality assurance practitioners to examine the intersection of research excellence and quality assurance systems. Panellists presented evidence on the impact of quality assurance interventions on institutional research cultures and discussed strategies for integrating research assessment within broader institutional quality frameworks.
9. Closing Address
[Photo: Closing Session]
The Conference concluded with a closing address that synthesised the key outcomes and commitments from both days of deliberations. Delegates affirmed their collective resolve to advance quality assurance across African higher education through sustained collaboration, peer learning, and the continued implementation of the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance. The Network looks forward to building on the momentum generated in Addis Ababa as it enters its next phase of institutional strengthening.
