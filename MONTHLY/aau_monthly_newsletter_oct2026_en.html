SECRETARY-GENERAL’S MESSAGE

The Secretary General’s Message

Dear Members, Partners, and Friends of the AAU,

I extend warm greetings on behalf of the Association of African Universities (AAU) and welcome you to the October edition of the AAU Strategic Highlights Newsletter.

The developments featured in this issue reflect the Association's unwavering commitment to advancing African higher education through strategic partnerships, institutional capacity development, research, innovation, and knowledge exchange. They also underscore the vital role of African universities in driving sustainable development and socioeconomic transformation across the continent.

Among the major highlights is the establishment of the AAU Representative Office in Russia, creating new opportunities for collaboration in higher education, research, and human capital development. This edition also features the launch of the €60 million Study and Research in Africa (SRiA) initiative, implemented in partnership with the European Union to enhance the quality, visibility, and global attractiveness of African higher education.

As momentum builds for the 2026 Africa Universities' Day celebration, themed “The Future-Ready African Universities: Anticipating Change, Transforming Education, Shaping Africa's Future,” we are reminded of the importance of positioning our institutions to respond proactively to emerging challenges and opportunities in an increasingly dynamic global environment.

Looking ahead, we are particularly excited about the 24th Conference of Rectors, Vice-Chancellors and Presidents of African Universities (COREVIP 2027), which will coincide with the AAU's 60th Anniversary. These landmark milestones provide an opportunity to celebrate six decades of collective achievement while charting a bold and transformative vision for the future of African higher education. I warmly invite you to register and secure your participation in this premier continental higher education gathering.

I encourage you to explore this edition, reflect on the progress being made across our higher education community, and remain actively engaged as we work together to build stronger, more innovative, and future-ready African universities.

Prof. Olusola Bandele Oyewole
Secretary-General, Association of African Universities

Association of African Universities Establishes Strategic Representative Office in Russia to Drive Higher Education and Research Cooperation

ARTICLE

The Association of African Universities (AAU) has announced the official establishment of its Representative Office in Russia, a strategic new hub created to deepen collaboration in higher education, research, and human capacity development between Africa and Russia.

The office will be hosted by the prestigious Empress Catherine II St. Petersburg Mining University and coordinated by the UNESCO Competency Centre for Mining Engineering Education under the auspices of Empress Catherine II St. Petersburg Mining University. The establishment of the office is underpinned by a five-year strategic agreement designed to promote partnerships on new international studies and projects, mirroring the AAU’s successful regional office model in Europe.

€60 Million SRiA Initiative Set to Transform African Higher Education

ARTICLE

African higher education is set for a major boost following the launch of the €60 million Study and Research in Africa (SRiA) initiative by the AAU and the European Union. The partnership aims to expand opportunities for study, research, innovation, and international collaboration across the continent.

Funded by the European Commission and running until 2030, SRiA is led by DAAD and AAU alongside European partners across multiple countries. The initiative forms part of the EU’s Global Gateway Youth Mobility Flagship and is built to strengthen the quality, relevance, responsiveness, inclusiveness, accessibility and attractiveness of African higher education institutions.

Sod-cutting ceremony in Mauritania marks a new phase in building a regional institution for stronger education systems, shared knowledge and lasting reform across the Sahel

ARTICLE

A vision for stronger, more connected and resilient education systems across the Sahel is taking physical shape.

The sod-cutting ceremony for the permanent headquarters of the EduSahel Institute in Mauritania, held on Monday, September 21, 2026, in Nouakchott, marks an important milestone in the implementation of the Sahel Regional Engagement for Learning and Collaboration in Education (RELANCE) project. More than the start of construction, the ceremony signals a transition from institutional preparation towards establishing a permanent regional platform for education research, capacity development, policy dialogue and cooperation.

EduSahel is being developed as a regional intergovernmental education institute with three core missions: applied research, training, policy advice and knowledge sharing.

CHINNOVA Advances an Evidence-Driven Approach to Climate and Health Research in West and Central Africa

ARTICLE

As climate change continues to reshape disease patterns and heighten the risk of infectious disease outbreaks across Africa, the need for evidence-driven research and coordinated action has never been more urgent. Meeting this challenge requires more than scientific discovery; it demands research that is shaped by regional priorities and translated into practical solutions. It was this shared commitment to advancing the One Health agenda that brought together researchers, policymakers, development partners, and public health experts in Conakry, Guinea, from 9-11 July 2026 for the International Workshop on the Presentation of Results from the DOPERAUS Project and Other IDRC-funded Projects in Sub-Saharan Africa. The DOPERAUS Project is an IDRC-funded initiative that seeks to operationalise the One Health approach by strengthening coordination, surveillance, and multidisciplinary collaboration to improve the prevention of and response to infectious disease outbreaks.

For the Climate Change and Health Innovation Hub for West and Central Africa (CHINNOVA), implemented by the Association of African Universities (AAU) in partnership with the West African Science Service Centre on Climate Change and Adapted Land Use (WASCAL), Institut Pasteur de Côte d'Ivoire, and Université Omar Bongo, the workshop offered a timely opportunity to demonstrate how the Hub is contributing to this shared vision. Various IDRC-funded projects presented findings from research conducted since 2023 on infectious diseases and epidemic prevention across Benin, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Guinea, Liberia, Nigeria, Sierra Leone, and Uganda. Alongside these presentations, CHINNOVA showcased how it is laying the foundation for the next generation of climate and health research, ensuring that investments are informed by regional evidence and aligned with the One Health approach.

Ninety-nine higher education professionals from 36 institutions across Africa have called for stronger digital presence, improved institutional data and responsible adoption of artificial intelligence to enhance the global visibility of African universities.

The call emerged during the third Association of African Universities (AAU) training workshop on “Positioning Your University for Global Visibility,” held from July 14-17, 2026, at the Kenya School of Monetary Studies in Nairobi, Kenya.
