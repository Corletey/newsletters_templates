Driving Digital Connectivity: AAU Launches eduroam

The Association of African Universities has taken a step in strengthening its digital infrastructure with the introduction of eduroam (education roaming), a secure global Wi-Fi service for the international research and education community.

The rollout reflects AAU’s commitment to improving digital connectivity, enhancing cybersecurity, and providing staff with seamless access to online resources both at the Secretariat and across thousands of participating universities and research institutions worldwide.

Using their institutional credentials, staff can securely connect to the eduroam network without the need for guest accounts or shared passwords. The service employs advanced encryption and authentication protocols to protect user information while providing reliable internet access at partner institutions during conferences, research collaborations, and official travel.

The service is currently being piloted with a cross section of Secretariat staff and will be expanded to all staff in the coming months. Speaking on this initiative - Dr. Songu, the new Head of IT said that "With eduroam now operational at the Secretariat, staff, researchers, students, and visitors from participating institutions worldwide can securely access wireless internet using their home institutional credentials. This eliminates the need for temporary guest accounts while ensuring robust authentication, enhanced cybersecurity, and an improved user experience.

Looking ahead, AAU will continue investing in advanced digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, cloud-based services, digital learning platforms, research data management, and trusted identity solutions that strengthen institutional resilience and improve service delivery to our member universities."

AAU Officially Launches COREVIP 2027 and AAU@60

The Association of African Universities officially launched the 24th Conference of Rectors, Vice Chancellors and Presidents of African Universities (COREVIP 2027) alongside its Diamond Jubilee celebrations (AAU@60) during a virtual event held on 28 July 2026.

The launch brought together university leaders, policymakers, development partners and higher education stakeholders from across Africa and beyond, marking the start of preparations for the Association’s flagship conference and the celebration of 60 years of advancing higher education across the continent.

Held under the theme, "Universities for Africa’s Future: Repositioning African Universities for Resilience, Relevance and Global Impact," COREVIP 2027 will provide a platform for dialogue on strengthening higher education through innovation, research, resilient institutions, ethical leadership and global partnerships.

The event also unveiled the COREVIP 2027 and AAU@60 visual identities, conference promotional materials and the roadmap to Nairobi, Kenya, where KCA University will host the conference from 20 to 23 July 2027.

Registration for COREVIP 2027 is now officially open after the successful launch, and staff, member institutions, partners and stakeholders are encouraged to secure their participation or share this widely within their respective networks as preparations gather momentum. HAQAA3 Agency Reviews Strengthen Quality Assurance Across Africa

The Harmonisation of African Higher Education Quality Assurance and Accreditation (HAQAA3) Initiative continues to advance quality assurance across the continent through its External Agency Review Programme. Working in partnership with higher education regulatory bodies, the initiative has successfully completed reviews in several African countries, including Lesotho, Namibia, Egypt, Guinea, Senegal, Morocco, and Angola.

The reviews have provided participating agencies with valuable opportunities for peer learning, institutional reflection, and continuous improvement while promoting the African Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance (ASG-QA). Emerging good practices include strengthening internal quality assurance systems, expanding stakeholder engagement, integrating digital accreditation processes, and enhancing transparency in quality assurance across higher education institutions.

As the initiative continues to support quality enhancement across Africa, the final round of applications for the HAQAA3 External Agency Review Programme is now open, providing additional higher education regulatory bodies with the opportunity to benchmark their systems and strengthen institutional quality assurance practices.

AAU to Support Regional Facilitation of the World Bank’s SIRA Programme

The Association of African Universities (AAU), through its Directorate of Special Projects, is expected to play a key role in the regional facilitation of the Skills for Innovation, Resilience, and Aspirations (SIRA) Programme, a US$642 million World Bank initiative aimed at strengthening education-to-employment pathways for young people across West and Central Africa.

Currently under discussion between the AAU and the World Bank, the programme is expected to benefit approximately 5.4 million young people, with a particular focus on young women and out-of-school youth in Cabo Verde, Côte d’Ivoire, and Guinea. SIRA will support the development of market-relevant skills and improve access to employment opportunities in priority sectors, including healthcare, agriculture, tourism, manufacturing, and energy.

Building on its extensive experience in coordinating regional higher education initiatives, the AAU is expected to support programme implementation through regional coordination, stakeholder engagement, monitoring and evaluation, and knowledge sharing. The initiative also complements the ACE Innovate Project, further reinforcing the Association’s commitment to strengthening skills development, innovation, and inclusive economic growth across Africa.

AAU and ICANN Advance Universal Acceptance Curriculum in African Universities

The Association of African Universities (AAU), in collaboration with the Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN), continues to make significant strides in promoting the Universal Acceptance (UA) Curriculum Initiative across African higher education institutions. The initiative aims to equip future ICT professionals with the knowledge and practical skills needed to build a more inclusive and multilingual digital ecosystem.

A major milestone was recently achieved when the Women’s University in Africa (WUA), Zimbabwe, became one of the first universities on the continent to formally adopt the UA Curriculum by signing a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with ICANN. This was followed by a two-day capacity-building workshop, supported by the AAU, which equipped faculty members with the skills and resources needed to integrate Universal Acceptance concepts into their teaching.

The initiative reinforces the AAU’s commitment to strengthening digital capacity, promoting internet inclusivity, and preparing graduates with the skills needed to support Africa’s digital transformation. The success of the Women’s University in Africa also provides a model for other AAU member institutions interested in adopting the UA Curriculum.

CO-CAT Project Advances to a New Phase of Institutional Capacity Strengthening

The Association of African Universities (AAU), in collaboration with the Association of Commonwealth Universities (ACU) and the Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine (LSTM), has reached a significant milestone in implementing the Climate-focused Organisational Capacity Assessment Tool (CO-CAT) project.

Following the successful completion of organisational self-assessments by 16 universities and research institutions, the project has progressed to a new phase focused on strengthening institutional capacity for climate adaptation research. The assessments examined key areas including leadership, strategic planning, resource mobilisation, organisational learning and stakeholder engagement, providing participating institutions with evidence to identify strengths and areas for improvement.

The next phase will promote peer learning, institutional mentorship and collaborative action, enabling participating institutions to strengthen their organisational systems, share best practices and enhance their capacity to deliver impactful climate adaptation research across Africa.

AAU Policy Section

Staff Development Policy

As the apex continental higher education institution, AAU continues to prioritize staff growth and capacity building through its Staff Development Policy, which provides a structured framework for continuous learning and professional development across the Secretariat.

Staff are encouraged to take advantage of training opportunities by submitting requests through the appropriate supervisory channels. Requests must align with individual roles, departmental priorities, and organizational goals, and clearly demonstrate expected benefits. To ensure operational continuity, only a limited number of staff from the same unit may be approved within a given period.

In line with Section 2.4 of the policy, staff who receive development support are expected to apply the knowledge gained, support knowledge sharing, and, where applicable, fulfill agreed service conditions. Approval remains subject to relevance, operational needs, and available resources.

Staff are advised to consult the full Staff Development document for detailed guidelines on eligibility and requirements.

Health & Wellness

Staying Hydrated at Work

Staying properly hydrated is essential for maintaining focus, energy, and overall well-being throughout the workday. Dehydration can lead to fatigue, headaches, and reduced concentration, which can affect productivity and performance. Staff are encouraged to drink water regularly during the day, even before feeling thirsty, as thirst is often a late sign of dehydration.

Simple habits such as keeping a water bottle at your desk, taking short water breaks, and choosing water over sugary or caffeinated drinks can help maintain adequate hydration. Increasing water intake is especially important during busy work periods and in warm environments. Prioritizing hydration is a simple but effective way to support both physical health and mental alertness at work.
